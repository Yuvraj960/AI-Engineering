# What about Andrew Ng's philosophy?

This is one of the most useful parts of the research.

Andrew Ng's modern teaching direction is telling us something.

His Agentic AI course does **not** begin with:

> “Here is LangChain.”

It begins with:

> What is an agent?

Then:

* task decomposition
* evaluations
* design patterns
* reflection
* tool use
* planning
* multi-agent
* production

And he explicitly teaches the patterns in raw Python before frameworks.

That suggests a very important learning principle:

> **Frameworks are implementation details. AI system design is the durable skill.**

That is exactly how I would structure your education.

# What about John McCarthy?

McCarthy's definition becomes surprisingly relevant to modern AI engineering.

He defined AI as:

> the science and engineering of making intelligent machines

and described intelligence in terms of achieving goals in the world.

Modern agentic AI takes this idea almost literally:

```text
Goal
 ↓
Reason
 ↓
Plan
 ↓
Act
 ↓
Observe
 ↓
Adapt
 ↓
Goal achieved
```

McCarthy also emphasized that intelligent systems need representations of knowledge and the ability to reason about what they know and do not know.

That connects directly with today's:

* memory
* retrieval
* tool use
* planning
* agent state
* uncertainty
* evaluation
* world/context representations

So studying classical AI ideas isn't obsolete.

It gives conceptual foundations for modern agentic systems.

# The most important conclusion from the research

The field is moving away from:

> **“Can you call an LLM?”**

toward:

> **“Can you engineer a reliable system around intelligent models?”**

The evidence is remarkably consistent.

OpenAI's current agent roles emphasize infrastructure, context, tools, memory, orchestration, permissions, observability, evaluations, reliability, latency and cost.

Indian GenAI jobs emphasize Python, APIs, RAG, agents, MCP, vector databases, evaluation, guardrails and production deployment.

Andrew Ng's latest course emphasizes agentic workflows, tool use, planning, reflection, multi-agent systems and evaluation.

Hugging Face teaches agents, frameworks, agentic RAG, function-calling fine-tuning, observability and evaluation. 

Full Stack Deep Learning emphasizes the production lifecycle around AI systems.

And Stanford's traditional ML curriculum has itself expanded to LLMs, RAG, fine-tuning, prompt optimization and safety.

So your objective should **not** be:

> “Learn GenAI.”

It should be:

# **Become capable of designing, implementing, evaluating, deploying, securing and scaling AI-powered software systems.**

That is the AI Engineering curriculum I would bet your career on in 2026.

One final point from McCarthy is worth retaining: AI isn't restricted to mimicking human methods; AI engineering is about finding computational mechanisms that achieve goals effectively. That mindset is particularly important in agentic systems: don't blindly imitate how humans work—design the **most effective computational workflow**.
